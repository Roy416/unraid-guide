About Plex & Jellyfin

  • When you’re streaming a video on a media server and you need to change the quality settings (due to poor internet or device limitations), that process is called transcoding.
  • Intel CPUs can have hardware transcoding, called QuickSync. This makes it way faster and more efficient to transcode. If you don’t have hardware transcoding and try to change the quality settings, it might cause you to buffer, or prevent additional users to stream.
  • This is a paid feature for Plex but is free on Jellyfin.

A media server is something that allows you to consume your legally obtained shows and movies. It also has functionality for photos, books, and music, but there are better options for those. Plex and Jellyfin are the two most popular options.

⚖️ Plex or Jellyfin

When I first made this guide 3 years ago, I recommended to use Plex over Jellyfin as it was more stable and everything "just worked." However, the landscape has changed. Plex recently introduced two changes making it a lot harder to recommend to newcomers.

  1. You cannot watch remotely without paying. Plex either wants you to pay for the Remote Pass ($30/yr) so only you can watch remotely or pay for the Plex Pass ($70/yr) so all of your users can watch remotely. This is obviously to make at least someone pay for a subscription. You could buy the lifetime pass, however..

  2. As of May 2026, the lifetime Plex pass tripled in price, from $250 to $750. That is not a typo. They usually have Black Friday sales, however with this price hike I'm not sure if they'd have a big enough discount to bring it down to a reasonable price. It also doesn't help that the list of features they have planned after introducing the price hike aren't anything of consequence.

Jellyfin on the other hand is totally free and open source, and includes the features that Plex makes you pay for. It has slowly and steadily improved over the years. However, it still doesn't have the same polish as Plex since people work on it in their spare time. But it's a lot better than what it was before. Another thing going for Jellyfin is the community involvement. There are a lot creative plugins being made that bring useful functionality.

🤷 My Experience

Plex: I bought the lifetime pass for Plex a while back at a more reasonable price. So that's what I'm going to continue to use unless they some how start messing with the lifetime users. The library refreshes instantly, metadata matches stuff well (even for niche Anime specials) and everything works as it should right out of the box. There was one problem with offline downloading on mobile/ipad, but I think that's fixed now. The downside I see for myself is the lack of new features that I might find on Jellyfin. I also couldn't get Tailscale to work with Plex, it seems they're making it harder to do..

Jellyfin: I've setup Jellyfin recently to get familiar with it in case I do need to jump ship from Plex. Playback worked great for the 1080p shows I watched on it. I did spend some time fixing hiccups such as Anime metadata not appearing properly or subtitles not appearing with foreign audio. Finding what media player to use on each platform was also energy consuming. As the media player from Jellyfin is very barebones, which is why you'll see many alternatives.

📌 TLDR; What should I use?

It's pretty hard to recommend Plex now to most newcomers. You could still use Plex if you only really consume media at your house and you don't plan on sharing it with anyone.

Jellyfin is free.99 and has all of Plex's premium features. It does require you to spend more time tweaking some things and may not work as well in some regards, but it's FREE.
