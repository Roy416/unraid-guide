### ❓ What is Port Forwarding?

**When you port forward something**, you’re opening a port, aka **creating an** **access point**. This access point allows you to access your services when you’re away from home. However, since you’re able to connect to the access point from the outside world, potentially that a hacker can do the same.

Of course, we will try to **secure the ports and minimize threats**, but **some part of the threat will still remain**. Like we'll put a strong bodyguard in front of your house to protect you from most threats. But if a super duper experienced MMA fighter wants to take him down and wants to steal out of your house specifically, we might be in trouble. I'm not trying to come off as an alarmist, I use this method personally for Plex and Nextcloud. 

### 🤷‍♀️ How to Access Port Forwarding

To access port forwarding we will need to access your router's settings, and we do that by finding your router's IP address aka the default gateway. On Windows, open the terminal and type `ipconfig` or `route -n get default` for MacOS, find **Ethernet adapter Ethernet**, and copy the Default Gateway.  

Enter that into your browser, and you'll be prompted for an user name and password. If you're using a router from your internet provider, it'll say the login details on the router. 
After logging in, meddle around in the settings to find the networking settings and you should see a port forwarding section.
